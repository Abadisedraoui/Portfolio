Oxford Premium: A Platform Walkthrough

Oxford Premium is a complex educational platform, with thousands of resources for teachers and students. I worked there as the sole UX/UI Designer, across several projects. This page focuses on the three I go into the most in Deep Dive mode.

This page mixes all three together into one short, visual walkthrough of the real platform. I've also worked on several other projects, you can see more of them on my home page.

PROJECT 01

Design System

I led the creation of a design system for Oxford Premium, as part of a bigger rebranding and platform redesign. The goal was consistency, better accessibility, and an experience closer to Oxford's UK platform, HUB.

PROJECT 02

Reorganising a Complex Educational Platform

Oxford Premium had thousands of resources and different ways of organising them. Through research and information architecture work, I helped redefine how teachers could find and use what already existed.

PROJECT 03

Accessibility Across a Product Ecosystem

I worked on a wider accessibility initiative, across product, content and development. Platform audits, screen reader testing and documentation, so accessibility became part of the process, not a final step.

Here's what it looks like when all three come together, flow by flow.

The same system, everywhere else

The "Professional Development", "Certificate Courses", "New Resources" and "News" sections all run on the same components. That was the point of building a design system in the first place: a single source of truth, so nobody has to reinvent a pattern that already exists somewhere else on the platform.

What it added up to

  • A validated information architecture, built around how teachers actually search, not how content was organised internally
  • Clearer navigation through the side menu, which cut down discovery time and the learning curve, something users had been complaining about a lot
  • A design system used as a single source of truth by design and development, reducing ambiguity and streamlining collaboration
  • Redesigned interaction flows, with closer alignment to Oxford's UK platform (HUB)
  • A shared accessibility framework aligning product, development, editorial, legal and multimedia teams
  • One consistent organisational model, applied across different product lines
  • A platform that can keep growing without losing coherence
  • Tested across devices and screen sizes. Mobile wasn't the priority, but it still adapted well enough to be comfortable to view and use
  • Built compatible with Bootstrap, to make handoff and implementation with development easier
  • Built following accessibility requirements from the start, not added on as an afterthought

What stayed with me most from this project: complexity like this doesn't resolve itself. You have to keep choosing clarity, flow by flow, decision by decision, and trust that it adds up.
