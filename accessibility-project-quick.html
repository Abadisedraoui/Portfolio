Accessibility Across a Product Ecosystem

I connected accessibility audits, screen reader testing and practical guidance across Oxford's platforms, interfaces and learning content.

Accessibility (WCAG) Screen Reader Testing Documentation Developer Handoff

At a glance

Making barriers visible

Accessibility barriers appeared across interfaces, learning platforms and PDFs. Understanding them meant looking at how content was structured, built and read with assistive technology.

As a UX/UI Designer, I audited products, tested with screen readers and translated findings into component guidance and documentation for product, development, editorial, legal and multimedia teams.

Role
UX/UI Designer
Scope
Platforms, interfaces and PDFs
Methods
Audits and screen reader testing

The decisions

Three decisions behind the work

01

Specify accessible components

I documented focus states, contrast, labels and interaction guidance in the design system, giving development a reference for implementation.

02

Test across environments

I compared documents and platforms using NVDA, JAWS and Microsoft Narrator, recording how behaviour changed across formats and tools.

03

Give each team usable guidance

I adapted recommendations to each team's work, from document structure and image descriptions to component behaviour and development handoff.

Evidence

From findings to practical guidance

The same content could behave differently across a PDF reader, a browser and a learning platform. I documented those differences alongside component requirements and proposed fixes.

Outcome

A practical reference for the teams

  • Accessibility reports linking issues, their impact and proposed fixes.
  • Documented screen reader tests across platforms and document formats.
  • Component guidance covering focus, labels, contrast and interaction states.
  • Recommendations adapted to editorial, multimedia, product and development workflows.
